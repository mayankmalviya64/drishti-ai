1 · The problem

Money leaks in the gaps between people, platforms and dashboards

An ad buyer's job is one loop: see → detect → diagnose → create → approve → act → reallocate. Today every step is a human hand-off, so the time from "a problem starts" to "the fix is live" is measured in days. Toggle the loop and watch how much a single dying ad burns before anyone acts.

Four failures underneath

They stack: a late, wrong scoreboard feeds slow creative fixes, and budgets stay locked inside each platform.

1 · Latency

Auctions move in milliseconds and results change by the hour, but people check dashboards once or twice a day.

2 · Fragmentation

Meta, Google and OTT each optimise inside their own walls. Nobody optimises across them, and each one says "spend more with me".

3 · Creative decay

Fatigue is a slow slide, not a crash. By the time someone notices, the ad has burned money for days, and a regional replacement takes days more.

4 · Measurement the trap

Every platform claims the same sale. Optimise on platform ROAS and the number rises while real profit stays flat. Drishti anchors everything to true ROAS from the brand's own orders [5].

3 · Meet the agents

Five specialists and one conductor, each owning one leak

Each agent owns one sub-problem and one goal metric, plus a counter-metric that stops it from gaming that goal. They hand work to each other: P1 cleans the data for everyone, P2 spots a dying ad and asks P3 for a replacement, and P3's new ads and P4's freed budget flow into P5, which allocates across channels. Tap an agent to see what it does.

Orchestrator · coordinates the agents, runs the "is it us or the world?" shock filter, grades the system with the Agent Health Index, and escalates to a human
P1 Signal Agent: the Data Trust gate · unifies platform data with Shopify, GA4 and payment-gateway orders, runs holdout tests, watches tracking. If the data breaks, every automatic action pauses.

How every decision flows

OBSERVERead every platform and the store's orders, every hour
CHECKIs the data trustworthy? Is it us or the market?
DIAGNOSEFatigue, competitor, tracking bug or season? Weigh the causes
DECIDEPick the smallest move with the best expected ₹ impact
ACT / ASKSmall moves run automatically; big ones go to a human
LOGEvery action is saved with its reason and can be undone in one click

Tiered autonomy: a leash, not a black box

AUTO

Small moves run by themselves

A budget shift under 10%, pausing an ad that has spent under ₹5,000, launching a variant that passed brand-kit and ASCI checks.

ASK

Big moves wait for a human

Raising the total budget, a crisis playbook, anything over the caps. Sent to an approval inbox on Slack or WhatsApp with the expected ₹ impact. No reply means the safe default.

ALWAYS

Logged, explained, reversible

Every action carries a plain-English reason and a one-click rollback. A kill switch freezes everything and returns to the last known-good setup.

6 · Data connectors

What plugs in, how, and how often

Every input has a public API or a scheduled export, so Drishti needs no new tracking. Read-only access comes first; write access (pausing ads, moving budget) is switched on only after a period in shadow mode, where agents log what they would have done next to what the team actually did.

1 · IngestAirbyte or Fivetran connectors, plus webhooks for orders and payments
2 · Store and modelBigQuery + dbt: one table of spend, orders and the true-ROAS model
3 · MeasureHoldout and geo tests; open-source MMM (Google Meridian / Meta Robyn) refreshed weekly
4 · Reason and actAn LLM with tool use runs the five agents and the Orchestrator inside guardrails
5 · SurfaceDashboard, Slack or WhatsApp approval inbox, and the audit log

7 · How Drishti grades itself

One number, the Agent Health Index, that a weak agent cannot hide inside

Each KPI is scored 0–100 against its baseline and target, measured as lift versus the untouched control group. Each agent score blends Outcome (54%), Leading (30%) and Quality (16%) KPIs, weighted with AHP [9]. The AHI then takes a weighted geometric mean [13], so one broken agent drags the total down hard. AHI = G × P20.30 × P30.20 × P40.25 × P50.25. G = 1 if the P1 score is 60 or more, else 0. The weights come from the brand's own data: 50/50 between the two goals, then split by where the waste window and the ROAS gap sit. After launch, Shapley values from agent-level holdouts take over [12].

"Is it us or the world?" The shock filter

Before blaming an agent, the Orchestrator runs five checks. If the market moved, it tags a Market Shock, enters Storm Mode (tighter caps, protect budget, send a plain-language briefing) and does not penalise the agents.

  1. Control group: did the untouched holdout fall by the same amount?
  2. Breadth: did all channels and agents dip together?
  3. Auction: did CPMs spike account-wide at the same time?
  4. Status: what do platform status pages and API error rates show?
  5. Calendar and news: does a sale, IPL match, festival, election or news event explain it?

Severity ladder: not every red is a crisis

Green · HealthyEvery agent 70+. Normal autonomy.
Amber · WatchAn agent at 50–69. It runs its own self-fix playbook within guardrails.
Red 1 · AlertOne agent below 50 for 2+ windows. The Orchestrator finds the root cause across agents.
Red 2 · Critical2+ agents red, or a goal KPI breached. Shreya must act within 1 hour; autonomy drops to approve-only.
Red 3 · HaltData Trust gate fails or spend runs 20%+ over plan. Kill switch: freeze and roll back.

Two anti-flicker rules: a KPI is scored only after a minimum spend and sample, and an agent enters red below 50 but leaves only above 55.
